      How to play

      The goal

      Four players share one board, one on each side (or two players, South vs North, if the host picks a 2-player game). Most points when the game ends wins, even if you were knocked out along the way. The game ends when the 10-minute clock runs out, or when only one player is left.

      Your tree

      You start with a little pyramid on your edge: those are your roots. Everything you build must stay connected to a surviving root through your own blocks, like the branches of a tree.

      Every piece you place has to touch one of your own blocks. Placing on your own edge doesn't count as touching anything.

      Pieces

      You hold 4 pieces. Pick one, rotate it with R, and place it where the preview turns green. Normal pieces are the 7 classic tetrominoes. The small ones (1×1, 1×2 and a little L, marked ★) are special: they fill the tiny holes nothing else fits.

      Lines and toughness

      Fill a whole row or column and it clears: every block in it takes a hit. Edges count too, except the corners, so your roots can be hit by lines crossing your edge.

      Blocks have toughness (HP): darker blocks are tougher. Your pyramid's bottom row starts at 3, its middle row at 2 and its tip at 1; every piece you place has 1. A block breaks when its HP runs out. Where a row and a column clear together, the block in the crossing takes two hits.

      Cutting branches

      If a block breaks and some of your blocks lose their path back to your roots, that cut-off part stops being yours. What happens next depends on which colours it touches:

      • Nobody's blocks: it explodes.
      • Exactly one player's blocks: that player adopts it, and it becomes theirs.
      • Two or more players' blocks: it turns grey.

      Grey clusters

      Grey blocks belong to nobody, but they still fill space and can be hit by lines. Grey is not forever: after every move a grey cluster is checked again. If it's left touching only one player, that player adopts it. If it's touching nobody, it explodes. A freshly cut-off piece that touches a grey cluster joins it before anything is checked.

      Points

      • +1 for each block your line clear breaks (anyone's, grey included).
      • +2 for each of your own blocks your line clear breaks.
      • +1 for each block that explodes after your move cut it off, whoever it used to belong to.
      • +1 for each block you adopt.
      • Turn-based only: +1 for everyone still in the game at the end of every round.

      A hit that only chips a tough block scores nothing. You are knocked out when you have no blocks left, but your points still count at the end. Every time a player is out (knocked out or out of time), the time left on the game clock is halved.

      Stuck?

      If none of your pieces fits anywhere, you get a shuffle: a new hand of 3 normal pieces plus 1 special piece.

      Turn-based mode

      Players take turns clockwise, with a 2-second pause between turns. You have a personal clock (60 s, only running on your turn): each move gives back 2 s, plus 2 s per line you clear. Do nothing for 10 s and your turn is skipped; run out of clock and you're out, and your blocks stay on the board, faded. Clearing a line gives you a special piece. The clock's maximum shrinks by 1 s every round.

      Real-time mode

      No turns: place whenever you like. After each piece you wait 3 seconds (watch the 3, 2, 1 next to your hand), unless it cleared a line, in which case you can go again straight away. There are no personal clocks. Special pieces only come from shuffling.

      Rainbow pieces

      The normal pool also has a 1×3, the 1×2 and the small L, and the only special piece is the 1×1.

      Every piece you get has a 5% chance of being a rainbow piece (rainbow outline in your hand). A rainbow piece can go anywhere, as long as it touches at least one block of any colour, not just yours.

      Once placed it becomes rainbow blocks that belong to nobody: they never join anyone's tree, can't be adopted and never turn grey. Breaking one with a line clear is worth +1 (never +2, even if you placed it). A rainbow group that ends up touching no other block explodes: +1 per block to whoever caused it. Great for finishing a line anywhere on the board.

      Handy

      Use ◀ ▶ under the board (or the arrow keys) to look back through every move since the start. Online, anyone can pause; only whoever paused, or the host, can unpause.
